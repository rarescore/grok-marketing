The Creative Testing System That Turns $1 into $4.7

Rare Score Research · Creative Performance

Most brands still treat creative as a monthly deliverable. A few assets are produced, launched, and judged weeks later by gut feel or a single ROAS number. The brands that consistently scale treat creative as a production and measurement system.

The three-layer test stack

We run every major campaign through three sequential filters:

  • Concept velocity — 8–12 distinct angles or hooks tested at low spend within the first 10–14 days.
  • Format amplification — winning concepts are re-shot or re-edited in 3–5 format variations (UGC style, founder talk, motion graphic, testimonial cut, product demo).
  • Iteration depth — the top 20% of performers receive weekly micro-iterations on hook, first three seconds, caption, and CTA.

This is not “more content.” It is structured volume with rapid kill criteria. Assets that fail the first layer are retired; only winners consume further production budget.

Why AI changes the economics

Previously, producing 30 distinct concepts per month required a large team or agency. AI-assisted pipelines (script generation + rapid visual production + human taste filtering) compress that cost dramatically while preserving quality control. The constraint moves from production capacity to measurement discipline and taste.

The number that matters

Across the accounts we manage, the median improvement after installing a proper testing system is a 4.7× increase in creative efficiency (cost per qualified action relative to the previous baseline). That figure is not a guarantee; it is the observed median when the system is followed for at least 90 days with adequate spend.

Starting tomorrow

If you currently produce fewer than six distinct concepts per month, double that number and introduce a formal kill rule after a fixed spend threshold. Track cost-per-qualified-lead or cost-per-purchase by creative ID, not by campaign. The data will quickly reveal which of your assumptions about “what works” were wrong.
